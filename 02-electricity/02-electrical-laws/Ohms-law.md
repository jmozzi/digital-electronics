# Ohms Law: `V = I * R`  
- often called the **voltage drop** across a resistor/component:  
	-> that is the voltage that is “used up” by that component
> The voltage drop depends on the **current flowing through it**.

**Example:**  
- 10 V source, 10 Ω resistor -> current: `I = V / R = 10 V / 10 Ω = 1 A`
- Voltage drop across resistor: `V = I × R = 1 A × 10 Ω = 10 V`

> Even if the resistor could handle more, the **voltage drop is exactly what the current and resistance dictate**.
> `voltage drop` = “how much energy per charge is used by the resistor,” and it **automatically adjusts based on current and resistance**.

- **V** = voltage drop across component (Volts)
- **I** = current through component (Amps)
- **R** = resistance of component (Ohms)

**Use it to:**  
- Pick resistor values
- Predict voltage drops on PCBs
- Ensure components aren’t overloaded

**Example:** LED with 5 V source, desired 20 mA → resistor = ( R = V/I = 5 / 0.02 = 250, \Omega )
