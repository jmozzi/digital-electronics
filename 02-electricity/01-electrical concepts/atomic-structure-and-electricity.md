# Atom level
`Nucleus`:
- neutrons (neutral)
- protons (positive): fixed number that determines `atomic number` (the element's identity)

`Orbit / electron shells`:
- electrons (negatively charged) are attracted to positive charge of protons
- much lighter
- can move from atom to atom (either leaving or joining)

`Valence Shell`:
- most outer ring / shell
- electrons here determine the chemical reactivity and electrical conductivity
- in conductive materials (like metals) `valence electrons` are loosely bonded and can travel easily from atom to atom
- e.g. Copper $\text{Cu}$, excellent conductor due to its single valence electron, very loosely bonded

`Ion`:
- negatively or positively charged atom
- more precisely: an atom that has gained or lost electrons, resulting in a `net electrical charge`

`Net electrical charge`:
- overall total after adding and subtracting something
- net electrical charge = total positive charge - total negative charge
- More electrons -> net negative
- Fewer electrons -> net positive

`Anion`:
- net negative electrical charge: electron surplus

`Cation`:
- net positive electrical charge: electron deficit

`Electricity:`
>Freely flowing valence electrons are not yet electricity; we need to control the flow. 
> **Electric current** is the _net, directed_ drift of these electrons through a material in response to an external force (Voltage).
