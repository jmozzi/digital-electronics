# Electrical Resistance (`Ohm`) - opposes the flow
- measure of how much a material **opposes the flow of electric current**  
- high resistance -> less current for a given voltage  
- low resistance (good conductor) -> more current  
- wire -> low resistance -> little energy lost as heat provided there is a component in the circuit to use the energy (e.g. resistor, light bulb, motor etc); without component, the energy goes into the wire itself as heat -> dangerous
- light bulb -> higher resistance -> electrical energy converted into light and heat

| Wire type                                    | Resistance | Current         | Heat in wire                                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Low-resistance wire, no load                 | very low   | very high       | lots of heat → danger                            |
| Low-resistance wire, with proper load (bulb) | low        | limited by load | minimal heat in wire; energy mostly used by load |
| High-resistance component (resistor, bulb)   | high       | limited         | heat is created intentionally in the component   |

> Electrical resistance is the **opposite of conductivity**, measured in `Ohms (Ω)`  
> Water analogy: resistors act like narrow sections of pipe, slowing down the flow

Use **Ohm’s law**: I = V / R

- Opposition to current
- Low resistance → high current for same voltage
- High resistance → limits current, dissipates energy safely
- Use resistors to **control current**, protect components, or drop voltage
